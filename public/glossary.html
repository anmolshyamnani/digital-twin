CardioTwin Medical Glossary

Cardiovascular & Digital Twin Glossary

Every clinical biomarker, pharmaceutical term, mathematical metric, and visual outcome used across the system — explained in simple plain English with what it shows on your screen.

🔍
📊 1. Core Clinical Outcome Metrics

The key numbers and percentages calculated when you test a treatment.

ARR
Outcome Metric
Absolute Risk Reduction
The direct arithmetic percentage difference between the patient's baseline 10-year heart attack risk and their new risk with medication.
What It Shows on Screen: If your baseline risk was 25% and drops to 7%, your ARR is 18%. It tells you exactly how many percentage points of danger were eliminated.
RRR
Outcome Metric
Relative Risk Reduction
The percentage of the patient's original baseline risk that has been successfully wiped out by the intervention.
What It Shows on Screen: An 18% risk drop from a 25% starting risk represents a 72% Relative Risk Reduction (calculated as (18 / 25) × 100%).
NNT
Outcome Metric
Number Needed to Treat
The number of identical patients a doctor needs to treat with this medication regimen for 10 years to prevent exactly ONE heart attack, stroke, or cardiovascular death.
What It Shows on Screen: A lower number is much better! An NNT of 6 means treating only 6 patients will save 1 life. (Formula: 100 / ARR).
10-Year ASCVD Risk
Outcome Metric
10-Year Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease Risk
The statistical probability (from 0% to 100%) that a patient will experience a fatal or non-fatal myocardial infarction (heart attack) or stroke over the next decade.
What It Shows on Screen: Displayed in the main gauge dial. Categorized as Low (<5%), Borderline (5-7.4%), Intermediate (7.5-19.9%), or High (≥20%).
Vascular Age
Outcome Metric
Biological Heart & Vascular Age
The chronological age of an idealized healthy person who would share the patient's current cardiovascular risk.
What It Shows on Screen: Shows if your arteries are older than your birthday. For example, a 48-year-old with a 63-year-old vascular age can rejuvenate their arteries back to 49 years with therapy.
Kaplan-Meier Curve
Visual Chart
Event-Free Survival Probability Trajectory
A time-to-event graph showing the percentage of patients remaining free of heart attacks across Years 1 through 10.
What It Shows on Screen: Displays two curves: a downward red line (untreated baseline) and an elevated cyan line (counterfactual therapy), illustrating cumulative life protection over 10 years.
🧪 2. Biomarkers, Lipids & Blood Tests

The laboratory values that measure cholesterol, particles, inflammation, and blood sugar.

LDL-C
Lipid Biomarker
Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol ("Bad Cholesterol")
The primary cholesterol-carrying particle that penetrates and gets trapped inside coronary artery walls, driving atherosclerotic plaque formation.
What It Shows on Screen: Your primary cholesterol slider. Optimal is <70 mg/dL for high-risk patients (<55 mg/dL for very high risk).
Target: < 70 mg/dL (or < 55 mg/dL with ASCVD)
HDL-C
Lipid Biomarker
High-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol ("Good Cholesterol")
Scavenger particles that remove excess cholesterol from artery walls and return it to the liver (reverse cholesterol transport).
What It Shows on Screen: Higher is generally better. Low HDL (<40 mg/dL in men, <50 mg/dL in women) is a hallmark of South Asian atherogenic dyslipidemia.
Normal: > 40 mg/dL (Men) / > 50 mg/dL (Women)
Triglycerides (TG)
Lipid Biomarker
Circulating Blood Fats / Glycerol Esters
Fats stored from excess calories and carbohydrates. High levels create small, dense LDL particles and promote arterial inflammation.
What It Shows on Screen: Elevated levels (≥150 mg/dL) trigger warnings and qualify the patient for Icosapent Ethyl (Vascepa) simulation.
Optimal: < 150 mg/dL (High: ≥ 200 mg/dL)
ApoB
Atherogenic Particle Count
Apolipoprotein B-100
A structural protein tag attached to every single artery-clogging particle (LDL, VLDL, IDL, Lp(a)). It counts the exact number of damaging particles rather than just the cholesterol weight.
What It Shows on Screen: The most accurate marker of atherogenic particle concentration. Critical in patients with high triglycerides where standard LDL looks deceptively normal.
Target: < 80 mg/dL (High Risk: < 65 mg/dL)
Lp(a)
Genetic Biomarker
Lipoprotein(a) ["L-P-little-a"]
A genetically inherited, highly dangerous particle resembling an LDL particle with an attached "apolipoprotein(a)" tail that causes both plaque buildup and blood clots. Unaffected by standard diet or exercise.
What It Shows on Screen: Elevated (≥125 nmol/L or ≥50 mg/dL) in 30-40% of South Asians. Triggers risk-enhancing multipliers in the digital twin.
Normal: < 75 nmol/L (< 30 mg/dL)
SBP & DBP
Hemodynamics
Systolic & Diastolic Blood Pressure (mmHg)
SBP (Systolic) is the peak pressure in arteries when the heart contracts. DBP (Diastolic) is the resting pressure between beats. High SBP causes endothelial shear injury.
What It Shows on Screen: Measured in mmHg (e.g. 140/90). Lowering SBP by 10 mmHg produces a ~20% reduction in heart attack and stroke risk.
Normal: < 120/80 mmHg
HbA1c
Glycemic Control
Hemoglobin A1c (Glycated Hemoglobin)
Measures the average percentage of blood sugar attached to hemoglobin over the past 3 months (90 days).
What It Shows on Screen: Identifies normal (<5.7%), prediabetes (5.7-6.4%), and type 2 diabetes (≥6.5%). Activates SGLT2i / GLP-1 RA cardiometabolic benefits.
Normal: < 5.7% (Diabetes: ≥ 6.5%)
eGFR
Renal Biomarker
Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (mL/min/1.73m²)
Measures how well the kidneys filter toxins and waste from the blood. Poor kidney function accelerates arterial stiffening and vascular calcification.
What It Shows on Screen: Used in the 2024 AHA PREVENT™ equations. Values <60 mL/min indicate chronic kidney disease and elevate heart risk.
Normal: ≥ 90 mL/min/1.73m²
💊 3. Drugs, Molecules & Interventions

The pharmaceutical therapies tested in the counterfactual simulation sandbox.

Statins
HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors
Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin, Simvastatin
The foundation of cholesterol management. Blocks the liver enzyme (HMG-CoA reductase) responsible for making cholesterol, forcing the liver to clear LDL from the bloodstream.
What It Shows on Screen: Intensity levels: Low (-30% LDL), Moderate (-38% LDL), and High (-52% LDL, e.g. Atorvastatin 80mg or Rosuvastatin 40mg).
Ezetimibe
Cholesterol Absorption Inhibitor
NPC1L1 Receptor Inhibitor (Zetia)
A daily oral pill that blocks the NPC1L1 transporter in the small intestine, preventing dietary and biliary cholesterol from being absorbed into the blood.
What It Shows on Screen: Adds an extra 19% reduction in residual LDL-C when combined with statins.
PCSK9 Inhibitors / siRNA
Monoclonal Antibodies / RNAi
Evolocumab (Repatha), Alirocumab (Praluent), Inclisiran (Leqvio)
Inactivates PCSK9, a protein that destroys LDL receptors. By saving these receptors, the liver clears vast amounts of LDL cholesterol out of the blood.
What It Shows on Screen: Provides a powerful 58% reduction in LDL-C and a 28% reduction in Lp(a).
Icosapent Ethyl
Purified EPA Ethyl Ester
Highly Purified Eicosapentaenoic Acid (Vascepa)
A prescription-grade, ultra-pure Omega-3 fatty acid (not standard fish oil) that stabilizes plaque membranes and reduces arterial inflammation in patients with elevated triglycerides.
What It Shows on Screen: Applies a proven 25% relative risk reduction in cardiovascular events based on the landmark REDUCE-IT trial.
Bempedoic Acid
ATP Citrate Lyase Inhibitor
Nexletol / Nexlizet
A prodrug that blocks cholesterol synthesis upstream of statins. Because it is only activated in the liver and not in skeletal muscle, it does not cause statin-induced muscle aches.
What It Shows on Screen: Provides an 18% LDL-C reduction and 15% MACE risk reduction (CLEAR Outcomes trial) for statin-intolerant patients.
SGLT2i / GLP-1 RA
Cardiometabolic Therapies
Empagliflozin, Dapagliflozin, Semaglutide, Tirzepatide
Breakthrough cardiometabolic medications that improve glucose, promote renal sodium excretion, reduce visceral fat, and protect against heart failure and kidney decline.
What It Shows on Screen: Simulates a 14% reduction in major cardiac events, drops HbA1c by ~0.85%, and reduces SBP by 3.5 mmHg.
🧠 4. Digital Twin & Simulation Concepts

Key methodologies and models used to build the predictive simulation.

Digital Twin
Core Concept
In Silico Physiological Model
A software-based, mathematical representation of a physical patient's cardiovascular system that simulates future clinical trajectories and therapeutic responses.
What It Shows on Screen: The central framework of the entire application that synchronizes inputs, risk engines, and visual outputs in real time.
Counterfactual
Causal Inference
"What-If" Interventional State
In causal statistics, a counterfactual answers: "What would have happened to this patient if they had received Treatment B instead of Treatment A?"
What It Shows on Screen: The right-side sandbox controls that let you test hypothetical medication combinations without altering the patient's baseline truth.
Ceteris Paribus
Confounder Isolation
"All Other Things Being Equal"
A scientific principle where every other patient variable (Age, BP, Sex, Smoking, CAC) is frozen at 100% static values while varying only one biomarker (e.g. LDL-C) to evaluate its isolated effect.
What It Shows on Screen: Ensures that the risk changes you see are purely caused by the specific drug or biomarker change, eliminating confounding variables.
CTT Meta-Analyses
Evidence Base
Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' Collaboration
A world-standard meta-analysis of over 170,000 trial participants proving that every 38.67 mg/dL (1.0 mmol/L) drop in LDL-C yields a linear 22% reduction in major vascular events.
What It Shows on Screen: The mathematical formula powering all cholesterol-lowering risk transitions in the simulation engine (HR = 0.78^(ΔLDL / 38.67)).
Atherogenic Lipid Triad
South Asian Phenotype
High TG + Low HDL + High ApoB
The signature lipid pattern in South Asians characterized by elevated Triglycerides, depressed HDL-C, and high concentrations of small, dense LDL particles reflected in high ApoB.
What It Shows on Screen: Highlighted in the Rajesh Kumar preset case, demonstrating why South Asians suffer premature heart attacks even with "normal-looking" total cholesterol.
🔬 5. Imaging & Arterial Physiology

Visualizations and anatomical indicators of plaque build-up inside heart arteries.

CAC Score
CT Imaging
Coronary Artery Calcium (Agatston Units)
A non-invasive CT scan score measuring the amount of hardened, calcified plaque in the coronary arteries of the heart.
What It Shows on Screen: 0: Low risk (multiplier 0.45x); 1-99: Mild; 100-399: Moderate (1.65x); ≥400: Extensive plaque burden (2.4x risk multiplier).
Lumen Stenosis (%)
Vascular Anatomy
Arterial Cross-Section Narrowing
The percentage of the internal artery opening (the lumen) obstructed by atherosclerotic plaque.
What It Shows on Screen: Rendered in the interactive 3D Vascular Lumen Visualizer, illustrating how lipid-lowering regression opens the arterial channel from 55% stenosis to 25% stenosis.
Fibrous Cap
Histology
Protective Smooth Muscle & Collagen Layer
The protective structural layer separating the soft, dangerous necrotic lipid core from circulating blood. A thin cap ruptures easily, causing sudden heart attacks.
What It Shows on Screen: Visualized in the histological artery cross-section showing cap thickening and stabilization under statin and PCSK9 therapy.
