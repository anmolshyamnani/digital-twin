CardioTwin Research Framework

How the Full Digital Twin System Works

A simple, plain-language guide explaining how physiological simulation works, how new pharmaceutical drugs are incorporated, and what clinical outcomes are produced.

💡 Executive Summary

A Cardiovascular Digital Twin creates a computer replica of a patient's heart and arteries. Instead of giving a patient multiple drugs over several years to see what happens, we test the treatments inside the computer simulation first. In less than a second, the system predicts how much the patient's risk of heart attacks, strokes, and blocked arteries will decline.

1. The 4-Step Simulation Pipeline

1 Digital Twin Creation

We take the patient's real medical biomarkers: Age, Blood Pressure, LDL Cholesterol, HDL, Triglycerides, ApoB, Lp(a), Blood Sugar, Kidney function (eGFR), and Coronary Artery Calcium (CAC).

These form the unique multi-dimensional fingerprint of the patient's cardiovascular system.

2 Baseline Risk Engine

Using clinical guidelines (ACC/AHA Pooled Cohort Equations & AHA PREVENT), the system calculates the patient's baseline 10-year risk of having a heart attack or stroke if no new treatment is started.

It also calculates their Biological Vascular Age (how old their arteries behave).

3 Counterfactual Simulation

"Counterfactual" means: "What would happen if we gave this patient Drug A + Drug B?"

The computer applies proven clinical trial response factors (e.g. High-Intensity Statin = -52% LDL, Ezetimibe = -19% residual LDL, PCSK9i = -58% LDL) to predict the new biomarker state.

4 Real-Time Remodeling

The engine calculates the new risk, the absolute risk drop (ARR), and the Number Needed to Treat (NNT).

It also generates 3D arterial cross-sections showing plaque regression and 10-year Kaplan-Meier survival trajectories.

2. How We Incorporate New Drugs

Adding a newly discovered or experimental drug (such as Phase 2/3 clinical trial candidates) requires defining only three core mathematical parameters:

Biomarker Delta (Δ)

The exact percentage by which the drug lowers specific blood markers based on trial data:

  • LDL-C Reduction: e.g., -45% for Obicetrapib
  • Lp(a) Reduction: e.g., -80% for Pelacarsen / Olpasiran
  • SBP Reduction: e.g., -8 mmHg for ARBs
Clinical Hazard Ratio (HR)

The overall reduction in Major Adverse Cardiac Events (MACE):

  • CTT Meta-Analysis Law: 22% MACE drop per 38.67 mg/dL (1 mmol/L) LDL drop (HR = 0.78).
  • Non-LDL Pathway: Additional risk reduction (e.g. REDUCE-IT 25% drop for Icosapent Ethyl).
Target Eligibility Filter

Rules determining which patient phenotypes benefit from the drug:

  • Statin intolerance
  • Elevated Triglycerides ≥ 150 mg/dL
  • High genetic Lp(a) ≥ 125 nmol/L

TypeScript Implementation Snippet

// Example: Adding Obicetrapib (CETP Inhibitor) and Pelacarsen (siRNA)
if (counterfactual.obicetrapib) {
  // 45% multiplicative reduction in LDL-C (from BROADWAY Trial)
  ldlMultiplier *= (1 - 0.45);
}

if (counterfactual.pelacarsen) {
  // 80% reduction in genetic Lipoprotein(a) (from HORIZON Trial)
  projectedLpa *= (1 - 0.80);
}

// Compute new 10-Year ASCVD Risk via Cholesterol Treatment Trialists formula
const ldlDropMmol = (baselineLdl - projectedLdl) / 38.67;
const hazardRatio = Math.pow(0.78, ldlDropMmol);
const newRisk = baselineRisk * hazardRatio;

3. What Are the Outcomes of This System?

The digital twin produces 6 critical quantitative and visual outcomes for clinical decision support:

1. Absolute Risk Reduction (ARR)

The direct percentage point drop in 10-year heart attack risk.
Example: If risk drops from 26% to 8%, the ARR = 18%.

2. Relative Risk Reduction (RRR)

The fraction of baseline risk successfully eliminated.
Example: An 18% drop on a 26% baseline is a 69.2% Relative Risk Reduction.

3. Number Needed to Treat (NNT)

How many similar patients must be treated for 10 years to prevent one heart attack or cardiovascular death.
Example: NNT = 100 / 18 = 5.5 patients.

4. Vascular Age Rejuvenation

Translates complex statistics into biological heart age.
Example: Rejuvenating a 52-year-old patient's vascular age from 67 years down to 53 years.

5. Arterial Plaque Remodeling

Visualizes coronary cross-sections: lipid core shrinking, fibrous cap thickening, and arterial lumen widening from 55% stenosis to 25% stenosis.

6. Kaplan-Meier Survival Curves

10-year time-horizon curves demonstrating cumulative event-free survival across years 0 through 10.
